       Requirements for the Discovery of Agents With Names (DAWN)
                    draft-king-dawn-requirements-02

Abstract

   The proliferation of distributed systems, Artificial Intelligence
   (AI) agents, and network services has created a need for
   interoperable mechanisms to discover entities across administrative
   and network boundaries.  Entities may include AI agents, software
   services, and other named resources that need to be found and
   characterised before interaction can begin.

   This document defines the requirements for Discovery of Agents With
   Names (DAWN) and sets out the objectives that a discovery mechanism
   for such entities must satisfy.  It describes what information must
   be discoverable, what properties a discovery mechanism needs to
   support, and what constraints apply to discovery in decentralised
   environments.

   This document does not specify any particular discovery protocol or
   solution.

1.  Introduction

   Modern distributed systems increasingly rely on the dynamic
   composition of services and agents that may not have pre-configured
   relationships.  For example, an AI agent may need to find another
   agent with specific capabilities, or a service consumer may need to
   discover providers that support a required protocol version.  Further
   use cases and scenarios are described in [I-D.kay-dawn-use-cases].

   In each case, an entity needs knowledge of remote entities before
   interaction can proceed: what they are, what they offer, and whether
   they can be trusted.  Such knowledge could be obtained through static
   configuration, but this approach is impractical at scale and across
   organisational boundaries.  Automated discovery mechanisms are
   therefore needed.

   Today, where automated discovery exists, it is typically handled
   through proprietary directories or platform-specific mechanisms.
   These approaches do not scale across organisational boundaries and
   create fragmented ecosystems where entities cannot find entities
   managed by other organisations.

   An interoperable discovery mechanism is needed that builds on
   existing protocols and tools, benefits from an established trust
   model, supports proven delegation and federation architectures, and
   allows organisations to independently publish discovery information.

   This document defines requirements that any Discovery of Agents With
   Names (DAWN) mechanism must satisfy.  It is informed by
   [I-D.akhavain-moussa-dawn-problem-statement] and
   [I-D.mozley-aidiscovery], and uses the terminology of
   [I-D.farrel-dawn-terminology].

1.1.  Scope of this Document

   The requirements in this document address what information must be
   discoverable about entities, what properties a discovery mechanism
   must support, and what architectural constraints apply.  The detailed
   requirements are set out in Section 3.

   Discovery, as used here, is the initial step by which a discovering
   entity obtains the public Minimum Discoverable Information (MDI)
   about an entity.  It applies within a local network, within an
   organisation, and between cooperating parties over the public
   Internet.

   These requirements are intended to be solution-neutral.  They do not
   require discovery information to be carried in any particular
   protocol element or data record.  Section 3.2 sets out how
   information may be provided by value or by reference.

   The following topics are explicitly out of scope of this document:

   *  Open-ended search, semantic matchmaking, ranking, and selection of
      entities;

   *  Capability information beyond the MDI, including catalogues of
      capabilities;

   *  All communication beyond the initial discovery process;

   *  Indexing and searching of entities across the wider Internet;

   *  Entity registration processes, including attestation and other
      security mechanisms for registration;

   *  Design, definition, and governance of naming systems for entities;

   *  Identity management, authentication, and authorisation of entities
      themselves (as distinct from the integrity of discovery
      information);

   *  Trust management and trust evaluation beyond the secure exchange
      of discovery information;

   *  Capability exchange and negotiation between entities;

   *  Task management and orchestration.

3.  Requirements

   The requirements are organised into the following categories:
   discovery scenarios, discovery information, trust and security,
   scalability and architecture, discovery protocol, and extensibility.

3.1.  Discovery Scenarios

   REQ-DISC-1:  A discovery mechanism MUST support the discovery of both
      specific entity instances and classes of entities that can perform
      a desired function.

   REQ-DISC-2:  A discovery mechanism MUST be applicable within a local
      network, within an organisation, and between cooperating parties
      over the public Internet.  Different mechanisms MAY be specified
      for different deployment contexts.

   REQ-DISC-3:  A discovery mechanism SHOULD support referral to another
      entity, such as a catalogue or directory service, that can further
      the discovery process.  The information such a service exposes
      beyond the MDI is out of scope.

   REQ-DISC-4:  Discovery is initiated from a name or scope known to the

      discovering entity.  A discovery mechanism MUST allow a
      discovering entity to express the discovery scope of a discovery
      operation in one or more dimensions, and SHOULD indicate whether
      that scope has been fully searched or the results have been
      truncated.

3.2.  Discovery Information

   REQ-PROP-1:  A discovery mechanism MUST define the Minimum
      Discoverable Information (MDI) that every discoverable entity
      provides.  The MDI MUST include at least the entity's type, the
      services it offers, its reachability information, and the
      communication protocols and any security parameters needed to
      interact with it.  Properties beyond the MDI are optional.

   REQ-PROP-2:  The MDI MUST be available by value.  Information beyond
      the MDI MAY be provided by reference.  A discovery mechanism MUST
      specify the maximum depth of indirection a discovering entity is
      expected to follow, and the behaviour when that depth is exceeded.

   REQ-PROP-3:  A discovery mechanism MUST specify the behaviour of a
      discovering entity when a reference cannot be used, distinguishing
      at least the cases where the reference does not resolve, where
      resolution is prevented by the discovering entity's own retrieval
      policy, and where the reference resolves but the referenced
      information is absent.  A finding of absence MUST be based on a
      complete result for the requested lookup; an incomplete result
      MUST NOT be reported as absence.

   REQ-PROP-4:  A discovery mechanism SHOULD allow a reference to a
      capability card or equivalent machine-readable description of an
      entity's interface and functions.  The format, content, and
      exchange of such descriptions are out of scope.

   REQ-PROP-5:  A discovery mechanism MUST indicate whether discovery
      information is static or dynamic, together with any validity or
      freshness information needed for caching and refresh.

3.3.  Trust and Security

   REQ-SEC-1:  A discovery mechanism MUST provide a means to verify the
      origin and integrity of discovery information, for example through
      digital signatures, and MUST be resilient to attacks that poison,
      corrupt, or replay that information.

   REQ-SEC-2:  A discovery mechanism SHOULD allow an entity to control

      the visibility of its properties to different audiences (e.g.,
      public versus organisation-internal discovery), and allow an
      operator to control which entities are discoverable in a given
      context.

   REQ-SEC-3:  A discovery mechanism MUST be designed to limit its use
      as a vector for abuse, including amplification, scraping, denial-
      of-service, or unauthorised disclosure of discovery information.

3.4.  Scalability and Architecture

   REQ-ARCH-1:  A discovery mechanism MUST be capable of operating in
      decentralised architectures without a single centralised registry,
      and MUST scale across a large number of entities and
      administrative domains.

   REQ-ARCH-2:  A discovery mechanism SHOULD allow organisations to
      independently publish discovery information without depending on a
      third-party directory.

3.5.  Discovery Protocol

   REQ-PROTO-1:  A discovery mechanism SHOULD be built in a modular way
      using existing Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) protocols,
      such as DNS and mDNS, where possible, filling gaps only where
      existing protocols are insufficient.

   REQ-PROTO-2:  A discovery mechanism MUST define a predictable entry
      point for discovery that is based on ubiquitous and interoperable
      mechanisms.

3.6.  Extensibility

   REQ-EXT-1:  A discovery mechanism MUST support the addition of new
      entity types and properties, including domain-specific extensions,
      through structured, versioned schemas that allow backward-
      compatible evolution without changes to the core mechanism.

5.  Security Considerations

   This document defines requirements for entity discovery mechanisms.
   It does not define a protocol and therefore does not introduce
   specific security vulnerabilities.  However, the requirements in
   Section 3.3 place security constraints on any solution that satisfies
   these requirements.

   Implementers of discovery mechanisms that satisfy these requirements
   should pay particular attention to the following concerns:

   *  The integrity and authenticity of discovery information must be
      protected to prevent poisoning attacks that could direct entities
      to malicious endpoints.

   *  Integrity protection of discovery information does not, by itself,
      authenticate a discovered entity as the live peer in a subsequent
      protocol exchange.

   *  The security properties that can be provided differ by deployment
      context.  A specification should state which of origin
      verification, integrity, and confidentiality it provides in each
      context it supports.

   *  Access control mechanisms should be considered to prevent
      unauthorised disclosure of entity properties, particularly in
      environments where entity metadata may be sensitive.

   *  The discovery mechanism itself must not become a vector for
      denial-of-service attacks against the infrastructure on which it
      is built.

6.  Privacy Considerations

   Discovery mechanisms inherently involve the publication of
   information about entities.  Implementers should consider the privacy
   implications of exposing entity properties, capabilities, and
   organisational associations.  In particular:

   *  Entities should be able to control what information is made
      publicly discoverable versus restricted to specific audiences.

   *  The discovery mechanism should not require the disclosure of
      information beyond what is necessary for a discovering entity to
      determine whether interaction is appropriate.

   *  A specification of a discovery mechanism should state whether its
      design resists enumeration of the entities it makes discoverable.
      Unless it does, the set of discoverable entities should be assumed
      to be public.

   *  Where entities represent individuals or process personal data,
      compliance with applicable data protection regulations should be
      considered.

7.  Operational Considerations

   Discovery mechanisms that satisfy these requirements will need to be
   deployable across public and private networks, across organisational
   boundaries, and across heterogeneous operational environments.
   Operators should consider publication workflows, update and
   withdrawal procedures, caching and refresh behaviour, observability,
   operational policy, and the impact of discovery traffic on the
   infrastructure used to support the mechanism.
